# State-of-the-Art Design Review: Comparative Evidence for "bls-stats", a Point-in-Time Acquisition and Custody System for BLS Releases

## TL;DR
- The design is **soundly placed within, and largely convergent with, established prior art**: its single-writer-over-immutable-storage plane split is Datomic's architecture (Hickey 2012), its manifest-then-atomic-pointer commit and generation-pointer compaction are structurally identical to Apache Iceberg's catalog pointer swap (Iceberg spec; Blue/Tabular 2020), its bitemporal change log is the ALFRED / Philadelphia-Fed real-time-data-set model (Croushore & Stark 2001; Stierholz, St. Louis Fed), its wire-vs-content hash and restamp-only outcome mirror WARC's block-vs-payload digest and `revisit` records (ISO 28500), and its clairvoyance ban is the feature-store "point-in-time correctness" property (Feast/Tecton docs).
- The single **methodologically weak or stale conclusion** is "no conditional PUT ⇒ plain Parquet." AWS S3 gained If-None-Match (Aug 20, 2024) and If-Match compare-and-swap (Nov 25, 2024), and — more decisively for the given non-AWS stack — Iceberg/Delta/DuckLake commit through an *external* catalog or metadata database that never needs object-store CAS, so a single-writer table format is fully available and would buy file-statistics pruning, schema evolution, and free ecosystem readers (`polars.scan_iceberg`/`scan_delta`). The rejection is *reasonable for a lock-free multi-writer store* but is over-stated as forced.
- The **highest-value improvements**: (1) add third-party attestation of `knowledge_time` (RFC 3161 timestamping / a transparency log) because the product's core claim currently rests on self-reported logs; (2) adopt or emulate a table format's file-level statistics for pruning; (3) close the durability gap (open issue 15) with 3-2-1 / LOCKSS replication given the demonstrated 2025–2026 fragility of BLS's own publication chain.

## Key Findings

**1. The design converges with named prior art far more than it deviates.** Nearly every core mechanism has an established analogue:
- *Single writer over immutable storage* = Datomic's transactor model (Hickey 2012). Datomic writes almost exclusively immutable data with "the mutable parts … few, tiny, and updated by only a single writer (the transactor)"; "Writes are run one after the other in a single process, the Transactor … you can go a long way with a single writer thread once you've separated reads from writes." The spec's plane split (unlimited lock-free capture writers + one leased ledger writer) is precisely this.
- *Manifest-then-pointer commit* = Iceberg's atomic swap. The Iceberg spec: "An atomic swap of one table metadata file for another provides the basis for serializable isolation." A row is visible iff its `release_event` is committed; the spec's "write observation Parquet named by release_event_id, then PUT one release_event as THE atomic commit point; crash before manifest PUT leaves invisible orphans" is Iceberg's exact atomicity story (Dremio 2025: "If the engine crashes after writing data files but before the pointer swap, the data files are orphans").
- *Generation-pointer compaction* = Iceberg/Delta root-pointer versioning; the `_current` object is a catalog pointer.
- *Bitemporal change log* = ALFRED's `realtime_start`/`realtime_end` and the Croushore–Stark vintage model.
- *wire_sha256 vs content_sha256 and restamp_only* = WARC's `WARC-Block-Digest` vs `WARC-Payload-Digest` and the `revisit`/`identical-payload-digest` record.
- *current_state projection* = CQRS read model / event-sourcing projection.
- *as-of query with no leakage* = feature-store point-in-time correctness.

**2. The "plain Parquet is forced" argument is the one stale conclusion.** Transactional table formats do **not** rely on object-store CAS: Iceberg commits via an external catalog transaction, DuckLake stores all metadata in a SQL database, and delta-rs supports explicit single-writer operation (`AWS_S3_ALLOW_UNSAFE_RENAME=true`). So a single-writer table format is available even on an S3-compatible store lacking conditional PUT. The spec's own logic ("once there is a single writer the concurrency control is doing nothing while its costs remain") is correct *about the commit-safety feature only* — it ignores that table formats also deliver file-level statistics/pruning, schema evolution, time travel, and free polars readers, which a single writer still benefits from.

**3. The knowledge_time integrity gap is the most serious unaddressed weakness.** The product IS the claim "we observed these exact bytes at instant t," yet that rests entirely on self-reported JSONL logs with no third-party attestation. The archiving and software-supply-chain communities solved this with RFC 3161 trusted timestamping and transparency logs (Certificate Transparency RFC 6962/9162, OpenTimestamps, Sigstore Rekor). None appears in the spec.

**4. The 2025–2026 BLS disruptions validate the project's premise and raise the stakes on durability.** Per CNBC (Aug 1, 2025), President Trump fired BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer hours after the July jobs report; the trigger was July's +73,000 gain plus a combined 258,000 downward revision to May/June, with Trump posting on Truth Social, "I have directed my Team to fire this Biden Political Appointee, IMMEDIATELY," and BLS confirming Deputy Commissioner William Wiatrowski as Acting Commissioner. Per BLS release USDL-25-1352 (Sept 9, 2025), "The preliminary estimate of the Current Employment Statistics (CES) national benchmark revision to total nonfarm employment for March 2025 is -911,000 (-0.6 percent)" — among the largest downward revisions since at least 2000. Per BLS employment-situation release USDL-26-0169 (Feb 11, 2026), the final March-2025 not-seasonally-adjusted benchmark miss was −862,000, and "The change in total nonfarm employment for 2025 was revised from +584,000 to +181,000 (seasonally adjusted)." Per Reuters (Nov 21, 2025), after the 43-day shutdown (Oct 1–Nov 12) "The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics said on Friday it had canceled the release of October's consumer price report… 'BLS is unable to retroactively collect these data,'" and "October's unemployment rate, which is calculated from the household survey, will never be known" (the November report was moved to Dec 16). Meanwhile a parallel data-rescue ecosystem (Harvard LIL's data.gov archive; the Data Rescue Project; End of Term Web Archive; EDGI) matured in exactly this window. bls-stats is a single-bucket archive competing in a landscape whose lesson is "lots of copies."

## Details

### (1) Where the design sits relative to published state of the art

**Real-time / vintage revision archives.** ALFRED (ArchivaL FRED, launched 2006) is the direct institutional precedent. Its data model attaches three dates to every observation — `date` (reference period), `realtime_start`, `realtime_end` — which is exactly the spec's `ref_date` vs `knowledge_time` split (fredapi docs; FRED API `series/observations`). ALFRED exposes `vintage_dates` and lets a caller "download data as it existed on these specified dates in history," the as-of query. Katrina Stierholz's "ALFRED: Capturing data as it happens" (Stierholz, St. Louis Fed) and Anderson (2006, FRB St. Louis *Review*, "Replicability, Real-Time Data, and the Science of Economic Research") are the architecture references. Crucially, ALFRED's own Help page confirms two limits the spec correctly anticipates: (a) release dates are imperfect — "If a release date cannot be determined either from the source or from our data providers, the date that the series was first available in … FRED … is used" — i.e., ALFRED sometimes *reconstructs* first-publication dates, which is exactly the spec's separate "knowability" table with no join into `knowledge_time`; and (b) ALFRED verifies vintages against the source "when possible" but "many sources do not maintain previously published estimates," which is the precise gap (BLS overwrites flat files in place) that motivates bls-stats. The spec's claim that ALFRED omits unchanged republications is consistent with ALFRED's revision-driven vintage model (a new vintage is recorded when values are revised, not on every unchanged refetch) — the WARC community made the *opposite* choice with the `revisit` record, and the spec's `restamp_only` sides with WARC, recording proof-of-continuity even when bytes are unchanged. This is a genuine, defensible deviation from ALFRED.

The Philadelphia Fed Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists (Croushore & Stark 2001, *Journal of Econometrics* 105(1):111–130) is the canonical academic vintage database. Its documented design choice is a **fixed snapshot cadence**: "our real-time data set now includes data as they existed in the middle of each quarter (on the 15th day of the month) … For each vintage date, the observations are identical to those one would have seen in published sources at that time." That mid-quarter cadence means the Philadelphia set deliberately *misses intra-period corrections* — a limitation the spec's event-driven capture (capture every distinct artifact at release) is explicitly designed to avoid. Croushore (2011, "Frontiers of Real-Time Data Analysis," *Journal of Economic Literature* 49(1):72–100) is the survey to cite for the field. Other vintage databases in the family — the OECD real-time historical database (Fernandez, Koenig & Nikolsko-Rzhevskyy 2011, Dallas Fed WP 96), Bundesbank, ECB/EABCN Euro Area Real-Time Database — all follow the vintage-snapshot pattern rather than event-sourced capture.

**Bitemporal modeling.** The spec's PK `(program, unit_key, measure_code, ref_date, period_code, knowledge_time)` and its as-of query (filter `knowledge_time <= D`, keep-last) is textbook transaction-time/valid-time bitemporality (Snodgrass 1999, *Developing Time-Oriented Database Applications in SQL*; SQL:2011 system-versioned tables, Kulkarni & Michels 2012, SIGMOD Record). The decision to **not** maintain `valid_to` intervals — because closing an interval means mutating a prior row, contradicting append-only — is the correct, literature-aligned choice for a transaction-time store; it matches Datomic's accumulate-only model and event sourcing. The as-of join itself is a named primitive: Polars `join_asof`, DuckDB `ASOF JOIN`, and the ML feature-store "point-in-time join." The feature-store literature is the sharpest formalization of the spec's "clairvoyance" ban: "Point-in-time lookups prevent data leakage … Training datasets must reflect only the feature data that would have been available at prediction time" (Feast/Tecton). XTDB v2 (bitemporal SQL) and kdb+ point-in-time tick stores are additional bitemporal precedents worth citing.

**Object-store commit protocols / event sourcing.** The fetch_log-as-WAL and current_state-as-projection is exactly Kleppmann's "Turning the Database Inside-Out" / log-as-source-of-truth (CQRS). The Delta Lake paper (Armbrust et al. 2020, PVLDB 13(12):3411–3424) is the reference for why object stores are hard: "their implementation as key-value stores makes it difficult to achieve ACID transactions … metadata operations such as listing objects are expensive, and consistency guarantees are limited." That paper motivates the whole design space. The Hive `_SUCCESS` marker and "write data then flip an atomic pointer" pattern are the low-tech ancestors of the spec's `_current`.

**Web archiving of overwrite-in-place sources.** The parallel to WARC (ISO 28500) is exact and the spec should cite it directly. WARC's `WARC-Block-Digest` (digest of the full record block, i.e., bytes as stored/received) vs `WARC-Payload-Digest` (digest of the decoded entity body) is precisely wire_sha256 vs content_sha256. WARC's `revisit` record with the `identical-payload-digest` profile — "may be used whenever a subsequent consideration of a URI provides payload content which a strong digest function … indicates is identical to a previously recorded version" — is the spec's `restamp_only`. **What storing bare blobs + JSONL loses relative to WARC**: WARC keeps full request/response headers in-band with the payload in one self-describing record, and has a standardized tooling/replay ecosystem (OpenWayback, pywb, warcio). The spec's split (blobs keyed by SHA-256 + separate JSONL) reinvents this and forfeits the tooling; adopting WARC as the capture container is a candidate improvement. The Memento protocol (RFC 7089, Van de Sompel et al. 2013) is the web-archiving formalization of the as-of query: TimeGate does datetime negotiation via `Accept-Datetime`, TimeMap lists all mementos and their datetimes — the spec's as-of query is a private TimeGate.

**Government-data preservation, 2025–2026.** This context both validates the premise and warns about the single-bucket risk. Per the Harvard Library Innovation Lab blog (Feb 6, 2025), "Today we released our archive of data.gov on Source Cooperative. The 16TB collection includes over 311,000 datasets harvested during 2024 and 2025 … the first release in our new data vault project to preserve and authenticate vital public datasets." Datasets were collected 2024-11-19 to 2025-02-06 (the archive has since grown to 17.9 TB / 311,604 datasets per LIL's search interface), and LIL stores each dataset as a BagIt package (RFC 8493, a Library of Congress standard) via its `bag-nabit` tool with checksums and cryptographic signatures for authenticity and provenance (Jack Cushman, LIL director) — note LIL independently reached for BagIt fixity + signatures, which the spec lacks. The Data Rescue Project (IASSIST, RDAP, Data Curation Network) launched Feb 2025; the End of Term Web Archive (est. 2008) and EDGI (est. 2017) are the precedents. This is precisely the "gold copy" ecosystem the spec's open issue 15 should engage. The digital-preservation standards to cite are OAIS (ISO 14721), BagIt (RFC 8493), PREMIS (fixity), and content-addressable precedents (git object model, Software Heritage SWHIDs, restic/borg). LOCKSS ("lots of copies keep stuff safe") and 3-2-1 backup are the direct answers to open issue 15.

### (2) What is methodologically wrong, stale, or unsupported

**(a) "No conditional PUT ⇒ plain Parquet" is the one stale/overstated conclusion (fix/adopt).** The premise was true for bare S3 before Aug 2024 but is now doubly obsolete: (i) per AWS "What's New" (Nov 25, 2024), "Amazon S3 can now perform conditional writes that evaluate if an object is unmodified before updating it … clients can now perform conditional-write checks on an object's Etag … by specifying it via the HTTP if-match header … reliably offloading compare and swap operations to S3" (the If-None-Match existence check shipped Aug 20, 2024) — though the spec's store is explicitly *not* AWS and may still lack these, so this point is informational; (ii) more decisively, table formats do not need object-store CAS at all. Iceberg commits by an atomic swap performed *in the catalog* — "most catalogs implement it using a database transaction" (Blue/Tabular 2020) — and DuckLake stores all metadata in a SQL database ("Every snapshot pointer that would be an atomic rename in Delta is a transactional UPDATE in the catalog database"). delta-rs explicitly supports single-writer S3 without DynamoDB: "If there is a single writer … we can live without atomic renames, and the option `AWS_S3_ALLOW_UNSAFE_RENAME` allows you to avoid instantiating the dynamo client." So the environmental constraint does not force plain Parquet. The spec's narrower claim — that *for the commit-safety feature specifically*, a single writer makes the table format's concurrency control redundant — is correct. But the spec conflates "concurrency control is redundant" with "the whole table format buys nothing," ignoring file-level statistics/min-max pruning, schema evolution, time travel, and zero-cost ecosystem readers (`polars.scan_iceberg`, `scan_delta`). Iceberg manifests carry "per-file statistics: file path, partition tuple, record count, and column-level min, max, and null counts … enabling file-level pruning during scan planning" — exactly the pruning the spec would have to hand-roll for QCEW-scale diffs. **Verdict: the plain-Parquet decision is defensible on operational-simplicity grounds but the stated justification is wrong; it should be re-argued as "we accept losing pruning/schema-evolution/ecosystem-readers in exchange for zero external dependencies," not "a table format buys nothing."**

**(b) knowledge_time has no external attestation (fix an error).** The spec's central epistemic claim is unfalsifiable by a third party. RFC 3161 timestamping "essentially binding the hash value of a user's electronic data with an authoritative time source" is the standard remedy; OpenTimestamps (Bitcoin-anchored) and transparency logs (RFC 6962/9162, Sigstore Rekor) are the tamper-evident-log analogues. Without one of these, a `knowledge_time` is only as trustworthy as the operator ("You could change your system clock, create a timestamp, then change it back"). Given that the whole product exists because a government agency's own timeline is now politically contested, self-attested timestamps are a material weakness.

**(c) Content-hash normalization boundary (open issue 13) is under-specified relative to WARC practice.** WARC defines the payload digest over the decoded entity body only; the spec's content_sha256 "after transfer-encoding decode" matches this, but WARC does not further normalize line endings/BOM — and neither should the spec, because normalization beyond transfer-decode would destroy the forensic fidelity that wire_sha256 is meant to guarantee. The spec's instinct (decode transfer-encoding, stop there) is correct and WARC-aligned; issue 13 should be resolved *against* further normalization, with any line-ending/BOM handling pushed into the parse layer, not the hash.

**(d) Durability is deferred (open issue 15) in a threat environment that just demonstrated the risk (extend scope).** A single S3-compatible bucket violates 3-2-1 and LOCKSS. The 2025 shutdown showing that data can be *permanently* unobservable ("October's unemployment rate … will never be known," Reuters Nov 21 2025) is the strongest possible argument that a missed or lost capture is irrecoverable.

### (3) Candidate improvements, ranked by expected value

1. **[fix an error] External attestation of knowledge_time.** Add an RFC 3161 timestamp (or OpenTimestamps/Rekor entry) over each `release_event` manifest hash at commit. Low cost, directly hardens the product's core claim. Highest EV because it converts a self-report into third-party-verifiable evidence.
2. **[extend scope] Durability/replication (close open issue 15).** Implement 3-2-1 with at least one cross-provider replica and periodic fixity audit (PREMIS). Adopt or coordinate with an existing gold-copy effort (Harvard LIL / Data Rescue Project) so bls-stats is not the only copy.
3. **[adopt a better method] Adopt WARC (ISO 28500) as the capture container.** Store request+response headers in-band, use `revisit` for restamp_only, and inherit OpenWayback/pywb/warcio tooling and Memento (RFC 7089) as-of replay — instead of bare blobs + JSONL. This preserves everything the current design keeps *and* the HTTP metadata it currently discards, at the cost of one dependency.
4. **[adopt a better method] Reconsider a single-writer table format OR explicitly emulate its file statistics.** If plain Parquet is retained, hand-roll min/max column statistics in the manifest so as-of and diff queries can prune partitions — this is the concrete thing the spec loses by rejecting Iceberg. If a dependency is acceptable, DuckLake (SQL-catalog metadata, no object-store CAS needed) is the closest fit to a single-writer-on-plain-object-store model and gives time travel for free.
5. **[fix an error] Re-argue the plain-Parquet decision on correct grounds.** Replace "a table format buys nothing under single writer" with an explicit cost/benefit that concedes the lost pruning/schema-evolution/ecosystem-reader benefits and justifies plain Parquet by zero-dependency operability.
6. **[extend scope] Cross-check provenance against ALFRED and the Philadelphia Fed real-time set.** The spec already ingests these as `provenance=external`; formalize a validation invariant that flags divergence between a reconstructed vintage and ALFRED's recorded vintage, since ALFRED itself sometimes reconstructs dates and both can be wrong.

### (4) Evidence bearing on the fifteen open issues and three closing risks

- **Issue 1 (www.bls.gov 403 mitigation) & Issue 2 (is download.bls.gov also restricted):** BLS policy explicitly permits real-time bot blocking: "excessive robot activity on BLS websites is prohibited. BLS will block robots … Blocking may occur in real time" and it "reserves the right to block robots that do not contain information that can be used to contact the owner" (BLS Privacy & Security Statement, bls.gov/bls/pss.htm). Critically for issue 2, download.bls.gov **is** subject to 403s against bare/default User-Agents — a documented developer report shows `HTTP status was '403 Forbidden'` fetching a `download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/…` flat file (blscrapeR issue #41), and community clients set descriptive, contactable User-Agents to comply. So the spec's assumption that flat files are the reliable channel holds only with a browser-like, contactable User-Agent; download.bls.gov is *not* unrestricted. The specific "Akamai WAF" attribution is unverified from primary sources.
- **Issue 3 (plain Parquet vs Delta/Iceberg):** see Detail (2a) — decision defensible, justification stale.
- **Issue 4 (QCEW artifact choice):** BLS publishes both the LABSTAT "en" flat files (QCEW in LABSTAT back to 2001) and quarterly "singlefile" CSV zips at `data.bls.gov/cew/data/files/YYYY/csv/YYYY_qtrly_singlefile.zip` (CSV back to 1975; per the BLS "Guide to QCEW Data Sources," "Less than 10 percent of QCEW data is available via the BLS publication database. QCEW provides full data set access via the use of downloadable files"). Exact per-file byte size is not published by BLS; a third-party estimate (armollica/qcew-data) implies annual singlefiles of order ~1 GB uncompressed each, so a quarterly singlefile is roughly tens of MB zipped / hundreds of MB uncompressed — within the 8 GB budget if diffed in streaming form. The singlefile CSV is the correct diff artifact.
- **Issue 5 (CES national 21-month NSA benchmark span):** confirmed by BLS CES FAQ (bls.gov/web/empsit/cesfaq.htm): "the final benchmark revision, affecting 21 months of previously published data and anchored to March of the previous year, is published with the January preliminary estimates in early February." Preliminary announced late August/September, final in February. The Sept 9, 2025 preliminary was −911,000 (USDL-25-1352); the February 2026 final NSA figure was −862,000 (USDL-26-0169). Sourcing for the 21-month span: cite the CES FAQ and the benchmark article (cesbmart.htm) directly.
- **Issue 6 (OEWS publication window drift) / Issue 7 (EP twice-yearly flat-file refresh):** not separately verified in this pass; recommend citing OEWS and Employment Projections technical documentation directly.
- **Issue 11 (JOLTS-STATE routine revision depth):** not separately verified; recommend citing the JOLTS technical notes directly.
- **Issue 12 (mass-change circuit-breaker threshold):** QCEW's own revision statistics give an empirical anchor — BLS states establishment counts "rarely change more than +/- 1%" and employment/wage levels "rarely change more than +/- 0.1%" from initial to final publication (bls.gov/cew/revisions/), so a per-program threshold well inside those bands (e.g., trip on >1% establishment or >0.1% employment change at a national/state aggregate) is defensible and evidence-based.
- **Issue 13 (content-hash normalization boundary):** resolve against normalization beyond transfer-decode (WARC-aligned) — see Detail (2c).
- **Issue 15 (archive durability/backup):** LOCKSS, 3-2-1, OAIS (ISO 14721), BagIt (RFC 8493), PREMIS fixity; the Harvard LIL and Data Rescue Project efforts are concrete partners/models.
- **Closing risk "a missed capture is permanent":** empirically validated — the 2025 shutdown means "October's unemployment rate … will never be known" (Reuters, Nov 21 2025), and BLS overwrites QCEW files in place at persistent URLs (documented reload: "On September 9th, 2019, QCEW reloaded the final 2018 and preliminary 2019 data files … The files created on September 4th, 2019 incorrectly included preliminary QCEW data," bls.gov/cew/notices/2019/). This is the strongest evidence for the M0 "capture-only first" build order.
- **Closing risk "silent correctness failure":** the feature-store literature frames this as training-serving skew that produces models that "fit training data well and underperform online" — the analogue here is an as-of query that silently leaks later knowledge; the ASSERT/FLAG invariant engine is the right mitigation, and point-in-time-join correctness tests (the feature-store standard) should be part of the test suite.
- **Closing risk "alert fatigue disabling invariants":** the errata-rate budget (~12/year) is the right instinct; anchor the mass-change threshold to QCEW's published ±1%/±0.1% revision envelope so ASSERT trips are rare and meaningful.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 (before any build — validate the premise-critical assumptions):** Confirm download.bls.gov behavior under a compliant, contactable User-Agent (issue 2) and lock the flat-file ingest channel; the BLS bot policy permits real-time blocking, so build in polite rate limiting and a contact string from day one. Ship M0 capture-only as planned — the 2025 shutdown and the QCEW in-place-reload behavior prove a missed capture is unrecoverable.

**Stage 1 (harden the core claim):** Add external attestation of `knowledge_time` (RFC 3161 timestamp or OpenTimestamps/Rekor over each release_event manifest). This is the single highest-EV change and should precede scale-out. Benchmark that would change the decision: if you cannot get a trusted timestamp within the capture window, fall back to batching manifests and timestamping the batch root.

**Stage 2 (fix the durability gap, issue 15):** Implement 3-2-1 with a cross-provider replica and PREMIS fixity audits; coordinate with Harvard LIL / Data Rescue Project as a second custodian. Threshold to escalate: any single-bucket-only period longer than one release cycle is unacceptable.

**Stage 3 (revisit storage format):** Keep plain Parquet for v1 but (a) re-document the decision on correct grounds and (b) hand-roll manifest-level min/max statistics for QCEW-scale pruning. Re-evaluate DuckLake once its single-writer story on your specific S3-compatible store is proven; adopt it if it removes hand-rolled compaction/pointer logic without adding a CAS dependency. Benchmark to switch: if hand-rolled pruning or `_current` compaction becomes a recurring source of bugs, the maintenance cost has exceeded the dependency cost a table format would impose.

**Stage 4 (adopt archiving standards):** Migrate the capture container to WARC (ISO 28500) to preserve HTTP headers in-band and inherit Memento/replay tooling; keep the content-addressed blob store as the dedup backend (WARC `revisit` handles identical-payload dedup natively).

**On polars under the 8 GB budget (supports Stages 0/3):** Polars shipped a new morsel-driven streaming engine (default-capable from ~1.31, actively hardened through 2025–2026) with spill-to-disk sinks; `LazyFrame.sink_parquet` "allows streaming results that are larger than RAM to be written to disk," and out-of-core joins/group-bys now bound peak RAM by partition size rather than table size. This makes the QCEW wide-form diff (millions of cells) feasible under 8 GB, but note two documented caveats: (i) `sink_parquet` memory regressions have appeared between versions (e.g., issue #22635, 1.24→1.29 for nested Parquet), so pin and benchmark the exact polars version; (ii) not every operation has a native streaming implementation, so verify the diff plan actually streams rather than silently falling back to the in-memory engine.

## Caveats
- The spec's environmental constraints (non-AWS S3 without conditional PUT, single writer, ephemeral 8 GB containers, fixed Python stack) are taken as given; the critique targets only conclusions *drawn from* them.
- **The final CES benchmark figure has a source discrepancy that must be resolved before publication.** The primary-source BLS release USDL-26-0169 (Feb 11, 2026) documents the final March-2025 **not-seasonally-adjusted** benchmark revision as **−862,000**, and the 2025 total-nonfarm revision from +584,000 to +181,000 (SA). A "−898,000 seasonally-adjusted" figure surfaced in one secondary chain but was **not corroborated in primary sources** and should not be used without direct confirmation from the BLS benchmark article (cesbmart.htm). Cite −862,000 NSA (or the SA figure only once confirmed from the primary release).
- Two other BLS technical facts are only partially verified from primary sources: the exact byte size of a QCEW quarterly singlefile CSV (BLS does not publish it; the ~1 GB/annual-file figure is third-party), and the BLS API "500 queries/day" limit (the "50 series per query" figure is confirmed on the official signature page, bls.gov/developers/api_signature_v2.htm; the 500/day figure is corroborated by multiple client libraries and the registration engine but the official FAQ page did not render on fetch). The "50 series × 500 queries/day" characterization in the spec is consistent with these.
- The specific claim that QCEW files "released 2025-12-19 [are] replaced 2026-01-07" was **not** verified; BLS's documented Q1 revision cadence is September → December → March/June/September (bls.gov/cew/revisions/), so the next scheduled revision after a December release is March, not January. Treat the December→January replacement claim as unconfirmed.
- Attribution of BLS's WAF to "Akamai" specifically is unverified.
- Several secondary technical sources (vendor blogs, Medium) are used for corroboration of well-established facts (Iceberg/Delta/DuckLake mechanics); primary specs are cited wherever possible (Iceberg spec at iceberg.apache.org/spec, WARC ISO 28500 via IIPC, RFC 7089, RFC 3161, Delta Lake PVLDB 2020 paper).
- Forward-looking or contested items (e.g., political motivations around the 2025 BLS firing) are reported as events with named sources, not endorsed interpretations.
